- Unix began in 1969 at Bell Labs, after AT&T withdrew from Multics, a large time-sharing project run with MIT and General Electric that Bell Labs had judged too costly and complex. Ken Thompson wanted a comfortable environment for programming, found a little-used PDP-7 and, with Dennis Ritchie and others, sketched a simple file system and wrote a small operating system on it. In 1971 the group persuaded Bell Labs to buy a PDP-11 by promising a text-processing system for the patent department, which gave Unix its first real users and the funding to keep going.
